### Socialize

**Give teammates enough to agree or object, and never post without being told to.** For a
new feature, a new service, a change to a shared contract, and anything the team has to
live with.

Posting to a shared channel is outward-facing and permanent. It notifies people, it gets
indexed, and it cannot be unsent. Draft, show, wait.

1. Work out whether this needs the team at all. A change inside one service that nobody else
   calls does not. A new service, a change to a published contract, a new dependency
   everyone inherits, or anything that changes how other teams do their work does. When it
   is genuinely unclear, ask rather than posting to be safe, because posting to be safe is
   how a channel becomes unreadable.

2. Decide what you are asking for, and say it in the first line. Agreement on an approach, a
   review from a named person, awareness of a change landing, or a decision between options.
   A message that does not say what it wants gets read and not answered.

3. Match the artifact to the audience:

   | Audience | What lands |
   | --- | --- |
   | Engineers on the same surface | the diff, the contract change, the decision and its cost |
   | Engineers elsewhere | what changes for them, and when |
   | Product or design | a screenshot or a short recording of the behavior |
   | Anyone deciding | the options, the tradeoff, and your recommendation |

   For anything visual, a screenshot beats a paragraph and a short recording beats several
   screenshots. For a new service, a short document outlives a message, and the message
   should link it rather than repeat it.

4. Write it short, and put the ask at the top. Three or four lines: what changed, what it
   means for the reader, what you need. Detail goes in a thread or a link. `unslop-prose`
   and `technical-writing` apply, and a channel message is the surface where padding is
   least forgiven.

5. Show the exact text before sending, and wait for a yes. Include the channel and anyone
   you would tag. This is the step that must not be skipped, whatever the instruction
   sounded like, because there is no version of this that can be undone.

6. Record what was agreed where it will be found. A decision reached in a channel is lost
   within a week. If it changes how the team works, it belongs in a decision record, which is
   the `consistency` skill's `record-the-decision` playbook. Link the discussion from the
   record rather than trusting the channel to hold it.

**Reply:** the draft, the channel, and who would be tagged, then stop and wait. After
sending, report what was posted and where, and what came back, including a decision that
went unanswered.
